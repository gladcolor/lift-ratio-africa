    How it works

    Larval source management often fails on logistics: teams cannot visit every water body, so the order of visits matters. This tool walks through four steps and puts a number on two of them.

    1. Find

    Start from a list of candidate habitats, each with a location. In the demo, the tool creates synthetic habitats beside real streets of the area you choose.

    2. Rank

    Each habitat has a score: a prediction of how productive it is. If you also have field counts, lift-ratio tells you how good the ranking is.

    lift-ratio = (area under the ranking's curve − 0.5) ÷ (area under the ideal curve − 0.5)

    The curve shows the share of larvae reached as habitats are taken in score order. Random order gives 0 and the ideal order gives 1. A lift-ratio of 0.35 means the ranking captures 35% of the possible gain over random order. It is not 35% accuracy.

    3. Route

    Teams drive between habitats along OpenStreetMap roads. Each strategy chooses the next habitat by its own rule. The route lift-ratio compares the visit order with the scores, so it shows how much of the ranking survives once travel is counted.

    Street search is the baseline with no map at all: a team drives every street of the area and treats the habitats it passes. The gap between it and the other strategies is what the map from the Find step is worth.

    4. Treat

    The result is the number of working days a campaign needs to reach a coverage target, for each strategy.

    What the simulation assumes

    • Constant driving speed, and the same time at every habitat.
    • Roads are two-way. A habitat is reached from the nearest road point, in a straight line.
    • Teams start together. By default they do not return to the start at night and their clocks simply run on; you can switch on a daily return.
    • Every visit succeeds. Access problems, breaks and re-treatment are not modeled.

    Your data and outside services

    Everything is computed in your browser. A list you load is never uploaded. The page asks outside services for three things: the background map (CARTO streets or Esri satellite imagery), roads inside the map area, and place names you search. No language model or other server is involved.
